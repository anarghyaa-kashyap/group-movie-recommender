# RL vs DL vs QML — Group Movie Recommender

Generated by `python -m scripts.compare_models` from the models registered in MLflow. Same offline protocol as NFR-06 (`scripts/evaluate.py`): real IMDb catalogue (5,487 films), 30 simulated groups of 3–5 members × 8 movie nights, evaluation seeds 7, 11, 23, averaged. DL and QML were trained on simulated movie nights from seed 1000; RL v2's settings were grid-searched on seed 99. Both are disjoint from the evaluation seeds.

- **NDCG@10**: how close the top 10 is to the group's ideal order (1 = perfect).
- **Precision@10**: share of the top 10 that is in the group's true top 5 % of the catalogue.
- **Reward**: the simulated group's rounded mean rating of the film it watched (the #1 pick); the RL bandits learn from it.
- **Learning gain**: mean reward of nights 5–8 minus nights 1–4. Above 0 = the model gets better as the group rates films. (Watched films are excluded afterwards, so the best matches get used up; a flat line is not automatically a failure.)
- **Median IMDb votes**: how mainstream the recommended films are.

## Interpretation

- **Ranking accuracy (NDCG@10):** best is DL (0.685). RL v1 0.671, RL v2 0.676, DL 0.685, QML 0.649.
- **Reward for the film actually watched:** best is RL v1 (3.94). First night: RL v1 4.00, RL v2 4.00, DL 3.37, QML 3.82.
- **RL v2 vs v1:** NDCG +0.005, mean reward +0.00, learning gain -0.034 → -0.011, p95 latency 152 → 123 ms. The result is mixed: v2 wins on one of ranking and reward but not the other.
- **Speed:** fastest is DL (p95 13 ms); all models are far below the 3 s NFR-01 limit.
- **Mainstream vs obscure:** median IMDb votes of the recommended films — RL v1 442,491, RL v2 454,449, DL 77,938, QML 371,352. The simulator scores a film's quality by its raw IMDb average and ignores how many people voted, so a trained model can gain NDCG by recommending little-known films with a very high average; the RL models' Bayesian rating discounts those on purpose. A much lower vote count than RL's is a sign a model is exploiting that (see the sanity check).
- **Learning from the group:** only the RL models update from a group's own ratings (per group, isolated). DL and QML are fixed after training and treat every group with the same function.
- **Data needs:** RL needs no training data. DL and QML need thousands of labelled movie nights; here they come from the simulator because the IMDb export has no per-user ratings, and they would not exist at launch.
- **What QML adds:** the QML model is hybrid — its six inputs are classical (content fits from the votes and public ratings) and the 6-qubit circuit learns how to combine them non-linearly with 42 parameters. It runs on an exact classical simulation of the circuit (checked against Qiskit); at this size there is no quantum speed-up, and on real quantum hardware every score would need many measurement shots and would carry noise.
- **Caveat:** DL and QML are trained on data from the same simulator they are evaluated on (different seeds), which favours them; RL v1's weights and RL v2's settings were only grid-searched.

MLflow: training/tuning runs in experiment `group-recommender-models`; one run per model in `group-recommender-comparison` (with this report and the JSON attached); registered models `group-movie-recommender-rl`, `group-movie-recommender-rl-v2`, `group-movie-recommender-dl`, `group-movie-recommender-qml`.
